ARCHITECTURE SUMMARY

The proposal requests a database migration for the production instance of the "Legacy Reporting Platform" (Version 1.0) to a new database environment.

POTENTIAL RISKS

• Technical & Data Integrity Risk: Due to the complete absence of details regarding source/target database engines, schema changes, and data validation mechanisms, there is a risk of data corruption, data loss, or schema/query incompatibility.
• Operational & Downtime Risk: The proposal does not state an execution strategy, cutover window, or rollback plan, introducing severe operational risk of extended service outages for reporting.
• Integration & Dependency Risk: There is no information regarding upstream data sources (e.g., ETL pipelines) or downstream report consumers, creating a risk of broken connections and interface failures.
• Security & Governance Risk: The proposal lacks information regarding access management, encryption, network isolation, and compliance standards for the new environment.
• Scalability & Performance Risk: Insufficient information is provided regarding the sizing, capacity, and performance baselines of the new database environment to assess whether it can handle existing or future workloads.

REVIEW QUESTIONS

1. What are the specific technologies, versions, and configurations of the source and target database environments?
2. What is the cutover and execution strategy (e.g., big-bang vs. phased, offline vs. live replication), and what is the expected downtime?
3. What is the documented rollback and disaster recovery plan if the migration fails in production?
4. How will data consistency, schema compatibility, and reporting performance be validated prior to and following the migration?
5. What are the security, identity/access management (IAM), and compliance configurations of the new database environment?
6. Which upstream and downstream systems, ETL jobs, and user interfaces depend on this database, and how will their connection configurations be updated?

SUGGESTED REVIEW FOCUS

• Specification Completeness: Requiring the submission of detailed architectural, infrastructure, and execution specifications, as the current proposal lacks critical technical details.
• Cutover and Rollback Strategy: Ensuring robust, tested procedures exist for data validation, downtime mitigation, and rapid fallback in case of failure.
• Compatibility and Performance Assurance: Verifying that legacy reporting queries and downstream integrations remain functional and performant on the new target architecture.
• Security and Compliance Alignment: Confirming that the new database environment meets enterprise security baselines and regulatory requirements.

ARCHITECTURE SUMMARY

The proposed change involves integrating the production Customer Data Platform (v2.0) with a new, unspecified customer analytics service via a "Database Integration." The initiative is owned by the Enterprise Architecture Team and classified as having a medium business impact.

POTENTIAL RISKS

• Technical & Integration Risks: The proposal specifies a database-level integration but provides no technical design. Direct database integration creates risks of tight schema coupling, bypassing application logic, and cross-system dependency. There is insufficient architectural detail to evaluate the integration pattern.
• Security & Governance Risks: Because the system is a Customer Data Platform, sharing data with a new analytics service introduces significant data privacy, exposure, and regulatory compliance risks. The proposal provides no information regarding data classification, access controls, encryption, or whether the service is internal or a third-party SaaS provider.
• Operational & Scalability Risks: Analytical workloads can be resource-intensive. If the integration involves direct queries against production databases, it introduces risks of resource contention, latency spikes, or outages. The proposal lacks performance profiles, throughput requirements, or workload isolation plans.
• General Assessment: The proposal contains insufficient detail to fully evaluate technical feasibility, resiliency, or operational risks.

REVIEW QUESTIONS

1. Integration Pattern: What specific mechanism will be used for the database integration (e.g., direct connection to transactional DB, read replicas, ETL/ELT pipelines, event streaming, or API-based access)?
2. Data Governance & Security: What specific customer datasets will be accessed, what data masking/anonymization will be applied, and does the destination service meet organizational security and privacy compliance standards?
3. System Performance: What are the expected query volumes, data transfer rates, and potential impacts on production database resources and latency?
4. Hosting & Vendor Model: Is the new customer analytics service hosted on-premise, within the enterprise cloud perimeter, or by an external third-party vendor?
5. Operational Resiliency: What monitoring, alerting, access auditing, and rollback/contingency plans exist if the analytics service fails or degrades database performance?

SUGGESTED REVIEW FOCUS

1. Integration Architecture & Decoupling: Validate whether a direct database integration is appropriate versus decoupled alternatives (e.g., event streaming, APIs, or an asynchronous data warehouse/lakehouse feed).
2. Data Protection & Compliance: Ensure all customer data shared with the analytics service complies with relevant privacy regulations and security policies.
3. Production Workload Isolation: Confirm that analytical queries cannot compromise the availability, responsiveness, or integrity of the core Customer Data Platform.

ARCHITECTURE SUMMARY

The Platform Engineering Team is proposing an update to the routing configuration for the Internal API Gateway (Version 3.1). The change is scheduled for the Production environment and has been classified with a High Business Impact.

POTENTIAL RISKS

• Lack of Proposal Detail: The proposal does not contain specific information regarding the exact routing rules, target endpoints, traffic volumes, or security policies being modified. A comprehensive risk assessment is not possible without these details.
• Operational/Availability Risk: Because this is a production change with high business impact, incorrect routing rules could lead to misdirected traffic, broken backend dependencies, gateway timeouts, or full service outages.
• Security & Access Control Risk: Routing updates can inadvertently expose private or unauthorized endpoints, bypass authentication/authorization layers, or disrupt audit logging if not configured properly.
• Rollback & Recovery Risk: The document does not specify a rollback procedure or fallback configuration in the event of production failure.

REVIEW QUESTIONS

1. What specific routes, path mappings, methods, or backend target services are being added, altered, or removed?
2. What testing was conducted in non-production environments to validate that existing traffic is not disrupted?
3. What is the defined rollback and disaster recovery plan if routing fails upon deployment to Production?
4. What specific systems, downstream dependencies, or user-facing services are affected that warrant the "High" business impact classification?
5. Are there associated changes to security controls, rate limiting, mutual TLS, or authentication tokens for the modified routes?
6. Is the deployment planned with a zero-downtime strategy (e.g., canary, blue/green) or immediate cutover?

SUGGESTED REVIEW FOCUS

• Configuration Diff Analysis: Detailed verification of the exact routing configuration changes compared to the current production baseline.
• Blast Radius & Dependency Mapping: Identification of all services and consumer applications impacted by the route changes.
• Deployment & Contingency Strategy: Review of validation mechanisms (health checks, monitoring, alerting) and the step-by-step rollback plan.
